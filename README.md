# GLOBAL_ROAD_ACCIDENT_ANALYSIS-Understanding_Patterns_Identifying_Risks_Saving_Lives
Global Road Accident Analysis uses Microsoft Power BI to explore accident patterns across countries, periods, driver age groups, crash categories and contributing factors. The project identifies key risks, examines road safety trends and provides data-driven insights to support preventive strategies and reduce accidents, injuries and fatalities.

Introduction

Road accidents remain a major global concern, resulting in loss of lives, injuries, property damage and significant economic consequences. Understanding the patterns and factors responsible for these incidents is essential for developing effective road safety policies and preventive strategies.

This project, Global Road Accident Analysis, explores accident occurrences across different countries, periods, driver age groups, crash categories and contributing factors.

Using Microsoft Power BI, the analysis transforms road accident data into interactive visualizations and meaningful statistical observations. It examines the relationship between human behaviour, environmental conditions, accident timing and road safety outcomes.

The central objective is to identify patterns, understand contributing factors, and generate actionable recommendations that can help reduce road accidents, injuries, fatalities, and associated economic losses.

Story of the Data

The dataset provides a broad perspective on road accident occurrences and their consequences across different geographical locations and periods. It captures information relating to accident dates, countries, road types, road conditions, weather, driver characteristics, contributing factors, vehicle involvement, injuries, fatalities and property damage.

The data story revolves around three major dimensions:

1.	Human Factors: Speeding, driving under the influence of alcohol, distracted driving and driver age.
2.	Environmental and Infrastructure Factors: Weather conditions, road conditions, road types and accident timing.
3.	Accident Outcomes: Fatalities, injuries, injury severity, vehicles involved and property damage.
   
The analysis seeks to establish how these dimensions interact and influence road accident patterns.

The major stakeholders include government and transport authorities, road users, public health organizations, law enforcement agencies, insurance companies, urban planners, researchers, and emergency services.

Their shared interest is to improve road safety, reduce accident-related losses, strengthen enforcement and support evidence-based transportation policies.

Methodology

The project adopts an exploratory data analysis (EDA) and Business Intelligence approach, using Microsoft Power BI to examine accident patterns and communicate findings.

The analytical process involved:

1.	Data Understanding: Identifying the dataset's structure, variables and analytical dimensions.
2.	Data Preparation: Examining the raw dataset to ensure accuracy, consistency, completeness, and suitability for analysis. The process involves data cleaning, transformation, integration, and validation to ensure the dataset is reliable and ready for analysis and visualization.
3.	Exploratory Analysis: Examining accident occurrences by time, country, contributing factors, crash categories and driver demographics.
4.	KPI Analysis: Developing summary indicators for accidents, casualties, injuries, fatalities, and vehicles involved.
5.	Data Visualization: Using cards, bar charts, column charts, donut charts, calendar, heatmap and line charts to communicate findings.
6.	Insight Generation: Identifying significant patterns and interpreting their implications for road safety.
7.	Recommendations: Translating the findings into practical road safety interventions.
	
The analysis is primarily descriptive and comparative. It identifies patterns and associations but does not independently establish causal relationships.

In-Analysis Observations and Findings

The Power BI dashboards revealed several important patterns relating to global road accident occurrences, contributing factors, driver demographics, accident timing, geographical distribution, and economic implications.

1. Overall Accident Statistics: The KPI dashboard recorded approximately 890,049K global accidents, involving 3.505K vehicles and resulting in 2.493K casualties. The reported casualties comprise 2.34K injuries and 153 fatalities.
These figures highlight the significant human consequences of road accidents, with injuries accounting for the largest reported proportion of casualties.
2. Over-speeding and Accident Occurrences: The analysis recorded 317,451K incidents involving over-speeding, compared with 232,303K incidents within the prescribed speed limit.
Over-speeding represents approximately 57.74% of the combined incidents across the two categories, while incidents within the speed limit account for approximately 42.26%.
This finding identifies over-speeding as a major road safety concern and reinforces the need for effective speed management, traffic monitoring and enforcement.
3. Accidents by Weekday and Weekend: The dashboard shows that 73% of recorded accidents occurred during weekdays, compared with 27% during weekends.
This indicates that approximately three-quarters of the reported accidents occurred on working days.
The pattern may be associated with increased commuting activities, commercial transportation, and higher traffic volumes during weekdays.
4. Accidents by Time of Day: The time-based analysis recorded 577.778K daytime accidents, 219.182K nighttime accidents, and 93.089K accidents during dawn and dusk.
Daytime recorded the highest accident frequency, followed by nighttime and dawn/dusk occurrences.
The findings suggest that daytime traffic activity may contribute to higher accident frequency, while nighttime driving presents additional road safety concerns relating to visibility, fatigue, and driving conditions.
5. Accident Distribution by Crash Category: The crash category analysis identified rear-end collisions as the most frequently recorded category, with 237.397K incidents.
Side-impact collisions accounted for 130.199K incidents, while head-on collisions recorded 116.197K incidents.
The dominance of rear-end collisions highlights the importance of maintaining safe following distances, improving driver attention, and adopting effective braking practices to reduce collision risks.
6. Global Accident Distribution by Country: The geographical analysis identified the United States as having the highest reported accident figure among the countries displayed, with 82.074M recorded incidents. Mexico followed with 32.755M, Japan recorded 27.564M, while Russia accounted for 22.022M.
The findings demonstrate variations in reported accident occurrences across geographical locations. However, differences in population, vehicle ownership, traffic exposure, and accident-reporting systems should be considered when interpreting these figures.
7. Accidents by Contributing Factors: The analysis identified speeding as the leading contributing factor among the three displayed categories, accounting for 110.118K incidents.
Driving under the influence of alcohol (DUI) followed with 99.098K incidents, while distracted driving accounted for 82.227K incidents.
These findings highlight the importance of responsible driving behaviour, effective traffic law enforcement and public road safety education.
8. Drivers by Age Group: The demographic analysis recorded 20.701K incidents involving adult drivers, 13.089K involving elderly drivers, and 5.641K involving young adults.
Adult drivers recorded the highest accident involvement among the three displayed age categories, followed by elderly drivers and young adults.
This observation highlights the importance of targeted road safety education and driver awareness programmes across different age groups. However, accident involvement figures alone do not establish which age group has the highest individual accident risk.
9. Property Damage Trend (1950–2024): The property damage visualization examined accident-related property damage costs over the period 1950–2024.
The dashboard observations indicate a steady increase in property damage costs over the period, $126K in 1950 and reaching a peak of $245,347K around 2020, followed by a subsequent decline of $162,828K around 2024.

This trend demonstrates the wider economic implications of road accidents, particularly vehicle repairs and property-related losses. It also reinforces the importance of preventive measures to minimize accident-related financial consequences.

Key Analytical Insights

The combined findings highlight six important observations:

1.	Speeding is a major risk factor: Over-speeding accounts for approximately 57.74% of the combined over-speeding and within-speed-limit incidents displayed.
2.	Weekday accidents dominate: Weekdays account for 73% of reported accident occurrences.
3.	Daytime records the highest frequency: Daytime accidents total approximately 577.778K.
4.	Rear-end collisions are most frequent: The category records approximately 237.397K incidents.
5.	The USA leads the displayed country comparison: Its reported figure is 82.074M.
6.	Economic consequences are substantial: Property damage costs show a long-term upward trend between 1950 and 2024, peaking around 2020 with the sum of $245,347K.
   
Collectively, these findings demonstrate the value of data analytics in identifying accident patterns and supporting informed road safety decisions.

Recommendations:

Based on the findings, the following recommendations are proposed:

1. Strengthen Speed Enforcement: Governments and traffic authorities should intensify speed monitoring through speed cameras, traffic patrols and appropriate penalties for over-speeding.
2. Improve Drivers Behaviour and Awareness: Public awareness campaigns should address distracted driving, drunk driving, fatigue and other unsafe driving practices.
3. Enhance Weekday Traffic Management: Traffic authorities should strengthen traffic management during morning and evening commuting periods to reduce congestion and collision risks.
4. Improve Road Infrastructure: Investment in road maintenance, street lighting, road markings, drainage and effective traffic signage should be prioritized.
5. Promote Vehicle Safety Compliance: Drivers and vehicle owners should be encouraged to maintain functioning seatbelts, airbags, braking systems and other essential safety features.
6. Develop Targeted Drivers Safety Programmes: Road safety initiatives should consider the different exposure patterns and safety requirements of adult, elderly and younger drivers.
7. Strengthen Accident Data Management: Governments and road safety agencies should improve accident reporting, data quality and statistical monitoring to support evidence-based policy decisions.
8. Reduce Accident-Related Economic Losses: Preventive road safety measures should be prioritized to minimize vehicle damage, healthcare expenditure, insurance claims and other economic costs.

Conclusion

The Global Road Accident Analysis project demonstrates how Business Intelligence and data visualization can transform road accident data into meaningful insights.

The findings identify speeding, weekday traffic activity, daytime accident occurrences and rear-end collisions as important areas requiring attention.

The geographical and demographic analyses also demonstrate the importance of understanding differences in accident patterns across countries and driver groups.

Beyond the immediate consequences of injuries and fatalities, the property damage trend highlights the wider economic implications of road accidents.

Ultimately, reducing road accidents requires a combination of responsible driving, effective law enforcement, improved infrastructure, vehicle safety compliance and evidence-based transportation policies.
